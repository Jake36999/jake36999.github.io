Why me and not a big agency

I've caught the detail that changes everything — and still lost the deal

A client once confirmed a trial order at 200 boards. My manager was in the room; we both heard it. Days later it turned out the real number was 200,000 — he'd been pricing against that volume the whole time. I requoted, got the price down from around £40 a board to about £13 within six days, and matched every requirement he'd set: local enough to send his own engineers, in-house testing, full supply chain visibility. He still went with an overseas manufacturer, by about 65p a board.

That's not a sales story, it's a systems one. The thing I caught wasn't the price — it was a number nobody had clarified early enough. A missed call or an enquiry sitting unread in an inbox is the same failure at a smaller scale, just with nobody in the room to catch it. That's what these systems are built to do instead.
